# Abstract — Identifying Conditions for Multimodal Applicability in Reserve-Force Mobilization Corridors

**Title.** Identifying Conditions for Multimodal Applicability in Reserve-Force Mobilization Corridors — A Songpa-to-Yangju Bugok-ri Case Study

**Abstract.** Korean reserve-force mobilization must move large cohorts to training sites within a hard time window, but operational data are restricted and the relative robustness of single-mode (direct bus) and multimodal (rail-bus) systems is unverified for external researchers. This study reframes the question from break-even search to applicability-condition identification: under what infrastructure conditions does multimodal dominate within the Songpa-to-Bugok-ri corridor? A SimPy discrete-event simulator built from OpenStreetMap is evaluated in four phases with paired Common Random Numbers equalizing road-failure, arrival, and traffic draws across modes. We report censoring-aware penalized makespan and quantile arrival KPIs, with Morris elementary-effects screening (k = 14, T = 100, 1,500 evaluations) and a counterfactual sweep over rail headway, last-mile fleet, and rail capacity. Under Δ = bus_only − multimodal (negative ⇒ bus advantage), Phase 1a shows Δ = −58.5 min at p_fail_scale = 0.0 and Δ P(arrive ≤ 1500 min) = +0.433 [+0.245, +0.622] at p_fail_scale = 2.0. Across 81 counterfactual cells, 0 are multi-dominant (54 bus-dominant, 27 inconclusive). Morris ranks passenger_volume (μ\* = 44.5), direct_bus_fleet_size (27.4), and dispatch_interval (20.8) as primary drivers — all supply-side. The multimodal applicability condition is absent within the examined infrastructure space.

**Keywords.** counterfactual lever sweep; paired common random numbers; quantile arrival KPI; reserve mobilization; rail-bus multimodal
